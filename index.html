Nine Service Pages. One Form. And Nothing That Can Tell You Which Enquiry Was Worth £8,000.

1
enquiry form across your whole website

You are booked nine weeks out on 50% margins. You do not have a lead problem, so we are not going to pretend you do.

What you have is a selection problem. Every enquiry arrives the same way — a phone call, from a page with no form — and nothing on the site can tell you which of them turn into the large jobs and which turn into a gutter clean.

All 9 published pages read 22 September 2026
First — What Is Actually Being Done Well
We are told you have an agency. Here is what they have got right, fairly
  • Your page titles are properly built. "Gutter Repairs | Somerset | The Gutter Doctor" — service, county, brand, on every page. Two of the three competitors we checked do not manage this.
  • LocalBusiness structured data is in place, with a postal address. That is more than Masters Roofing or Byron's have.
  • Five tappable phone links on every service page, and you publish a landline as well as a mobile. Both of your nearest rivals publish a mobile only, which quietly costs them commercial work.
  • Google Analytics 4 is installed and running.

This is not a broken website. It is a competent small site that stops exactly where the interesting work starts.

Finding 1 — The Measurement Stack Is One Tag
Everything installed across all nine pages: Google Analytics 4 G-L2CDELBNVG Google Tag Manager not found Google Ads tag not found Meta pixel not found Call tracking not found Conversion values nothing to set them on
GA4 on its own tells you how many people arrived. It cannot tell you what they were worth.

You are a business where a gutter clean and a full fascia, soffit and guttering replacement are the same enquiry until somebody picks up the phone. One is worth a couple of hundred pounds; the other is thousands.

With no call tracking and one form, every one of those arrives as an anonymous phone call, and the analytics can never be joined to the job. If you asked your agency today which pages produce your best work, there is no data that could answer it.

No pixel also means you cannot advertise to anyone who has already looked at your site — and since nobody in Somerset is doing that either, it is sitting there unclaimed.

Finding 2 — Nine Service Pages, No Way To Enquire From Any Of Them
Forms, page by page: /gutter-repairs 0 546 words /guttering-and-downpipes 0 524 words /gutter-cleaning 0 444 words /fascias-and-soffits 0 509 words /bargeboards 0 546 words /cladding 0 586 words /painting-services 0 403 words /projects 0 352 words /contact 1 311 words
The form is on the page somebody visits last

Somebody reads 509 words about fascias and soffits, decides you are the right firm, and then has to navigate to a separate page to do anything. A form on each service page would also let you ask what the job is before it reaches you — property type, which service, rough size, timescale.

At nine weeks booked, that is the difference between fielding every call yourself and seeing the big jobs at the top of the list.

Finding 3 — You Serve Somerset And Only Name Two Places In It
Place names across your entire site: "Somerset" 81 mentions "Taunton" 22 mentions Bridgwater none Yeovil none Wellington none Minehead none Glastonbury none Frome none Chard none Pages targeting a specific town: 0
"Somerset" is a county, and nobody searches for a county when their gutter is overflowing

They search for their town. Pepper Roofing in Bridgwater already publishes seventeen pages and names Taunton on them — they are coming towards you, not the other way round.

This is the most straightforward growth left on the site, and it is the sort of thing an agency should have raised without being asked.

Finding 4 — No Reviews On The Site, And No Review Markup
AggregateRating schema not found Review schema not found Named customer reviews none found on any page Pepper Roofing (Bridgwater) publishes: AggregateRating + Offer + GeoCoordinates 7 reviews on the page
A smaller competitor is showing star ratings in search results and you are not

Review markup is what makes stars eligible to appear under your listing on Google. Pepper has it with seven reviews. You have none, on a business turning over a million pounds.

That is not a reflection of your work. It is a reflection of nobody having been asked to put it on the site.

What We Would Do, And Why It Is Not "More Leads"
  • Put call tracking and proper conversion values in. Until a phone call can be joined to a page and a job value, nobody — us, your current agency, or you — can say what the website is worth.
  • A qualifying form on every service page. Service, property type, rough size, timescale. So the £8,000 job is visible before you ring back, not after.
  • Build the towns you actually serve — Bridgwater, Yeovil, Wellington, Minehead, Glastonbury.
  • Put your reviews on the site with the markup so they can show as stars in search.
  • Then, and only then, decide about advertising. At nine weeks booked you may not want more volume at all — you may want to raise prices and pick better work. That is a legitimate answer and the data would support it.

At nine weeks booked and 50% margins, the question is not how to get more calls.

It is which calls to take. Nothing you currently have can answer that.
